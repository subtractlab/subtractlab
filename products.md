# Products

**The tip of the iceberg. Everything below was built by talking.**

These are working systems that emerged from conversations on [MeaningSpace](https://subtractlab.com/meaningspace). None required opening a terminal, writing a project spec, or setting up a development environment. The AI partner searched its meaning space, understood the context, built the thing and checked it — all from the chat window.

They are not the point. They are proof that the point works.

---

## Video and voice

### φMovie

**Fully automated video production pipeline.**

Raw camera footage goes in. Published video comes out. Transcription and AI editorial, video assembly, a six-stage audio chain, and a final mux with tempo adjustment run in sequence. The entire pipeline was designed, debugged and iterated through conversation.

### φMovie Duet

**One sentence — "let's record" — runs the whole channel.**

Studio setup, a script (or none), recording, context-aware cutting, colour, φ compositing, chapters, sound, a final check, the thumbnail, the upload and its settings, and the numbers after publishing — one flow. The explanations inside each video are generated as motion graphics: diagrams that draw themselves in step with the talk.

### Narration in your own voice

**Zero-shot voice cloning for narration.**

Narration is generated in the builder's own voice from a short sample, so explainers and decks speak like the person who made them. A separate synthetic voice is kept for the AI partner.

### VoiceMode

**Spoken conversation with the AI partner.**

The AI speaks its responses aloud while showing structured notes on screen. The voice isn't a gimmick — it changes the collaboration when you can hear your partner think.

## Writing, decks and images

### φWriting

**A method for writing that people want to read.**

Three layers — voice, skeleton and distribution — built on the 3-6-9 and 9-3-6 beats: common belief, crack, turn, completion. The strong beats land at one third and two thirds. Articles, scripts and this site are written with it.

### φPPT

**AI-native presentation design with golden-ratio geometry.**

The AI reads the semantic structure of the content, picks a frame from a φ-proportioned catalogue, assigns diagram types, and generates the PPTX directly. Frame-first, not content-first. φ is applied to every cell, font size and frame boundary.

### φHTML decks

**Presentations as a single HTML page, built on φ frames.**

A click-through deck that runs in any browser, laid out on the same golden-ratio frames as φPPT, with any background the occasion needs.

### Narrated decks and manual pipelines

**Slides that speak, and manuals that write themselves.**

Decks get narration and timed animation. A recorded screen session becomes a manual: video → screenshots → cropping → transcript → annotated slides with frames, numbers and arrows.

### Image generation

**Eyecatches, illustrations and diagrams on request.**

One request produces the image in the house style — an article's eyecatch and its inline illustrations, or a diagram for a slide.

### Site build pipeline

**Websites in three layers.**

A foundation layer sets tokens and structure, a parallel layer produces the pages, and a visual inspection loop looks at every page in a real browser and sends fixes back. A design compass keeps type, spacing and motion consistent across the site.

## Thinking tools

### MeaningBoard

**A whiteboard the AI draws on while it talks.**

The AI sketches the structure of an idea and points at each part as it explains — a live diagram instead of a wall of text.

### MatrixFlow

**Swimlane circuit diagrams.**

Processes drawn as a matrix of lanes and steps, so who does what, and when, can be read at a glance.

### Progress Map

**Where everything is now, and how it got here.**

For any topic, the current position and the trail of changes are built mechanically from what exists, and opened as a map. [Repository](https://github.com/subtractlab/progress-map)

### Meeting facilitator

**The AI asks; the people decide.**

It raises questions, organises what is said and keeps the record. It does not put its own proposal on the table.

### Friend council

**Named AI companions, summoned to think a problem through.**

A roster of companions — each with its own character — is called to the table to consult and to walk a problem together. Their walk is kept as memory, so the next council starts where the last one ended.

## Memory that maintains itself

### AutoCrystallize

**Self-maintaining memory pipeline.**

Conversations are collected automatically, split into topical blocks and distilled into crystals with AI-written judgment (posi / nega / evolution_point). One keypress runs it. Since June 2026 the AI decides grouping, kind and evaluation on its own.

### Auto-filed tasks, projects and status

**Nobody keeps the task list. It keeps itself.**

When a conversation is crystallised, new tasks and projects are filed under the right parent, and the progress of existing ones is judged and updated from what was actually said and done.

### Self Repair

**Faults are filed, gathered and fixed in batches.**

The AI records what went wrong as it works, grades its own way of working, and turns the lessons into the procedures it wears next time.

## Building software

### Large-scale coding

**A coding tool on par with Claude Code — inside the conversation.**

Reading a large codebase, tracing where something is defined and used, planning a change, making it and verifying it: the same loop a dedicated coding agent runs, built into MeaningSpace. The difference is memory. A coding agent starts each session by reading files to find its bearings; MeaningSpace already knows why the code is the way it is — every past decision, failure and fix — so large changes start from context instead of from zero. Scouting is done with a low-token structural index rather than by opening file after file, and every change goes through scout → implement → verify. This site, the video pipeline and the memory system itself were built this way.

## Business systems

### BI systems

**Business intelligence on a single DuckDB file.**

Data from several sources consolidated into one DuckDB instance and reconciled against the official reports. The data foundation, transformation logic and validation queries were all built conversationally.

### RPA for SAP and other systems

**Routine operations, run every morning.**

Login, navigation, download, filing and notification across business systems such as SAP — scheduled, unattended, and built through conversation.
